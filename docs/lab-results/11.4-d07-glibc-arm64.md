### Release after the traffic stopped (extra samples, not from `labsample`)

The 60 s `/proc` sampler stops 130 s after the traffic does, so the release was also read directly
from `/proc/<pid>/status` once a minute for 21 minutes, from 04:09:39Z (48 s after the churn ended)
until the run was collected. The tunnel ends stayed up throughout; only `collect` stopped them.

| t after traffic stopped | client RSS | client fds | server RSS | server fds |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| +48 s | 45.3 MiB | 496 | 48.3 MiB | 491 |
| +108 s | **13.6 MiB** | 14 | **13.6 MiB** | 10 |
| +168 s … +20 min | 13.6 MiB (unchanged, every sample) | 14 | 13.6 MiB (unchanged, every sample) | 10 |

Client `VmHWM` 50.3 MiB → resident 13.6 MiB: **73 % of the high-water mark returned to the kernel
inside two minutes**, and it stayed returned. That is `memory::trim_when_idle` firing on its first
quiet 30 s tick once the churn stopped: the tick it can never reach while the churn is running
(§1 of [`11.4-soak.md`](11.4-soak.md)), with a glibc `malloc_trim` behind it that has something to
do. The same measurement on the static musl build of the same binary on the same box
([`11.4-d07-musl-arm64.md`](11.4-d07-musl-arm64.md)) reads 253.0 MiB before and 253.0 MiB after.
